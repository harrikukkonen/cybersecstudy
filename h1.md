## B) Review the review
Review: The economic dimensions of cloud security: A systematic literature review - Jufo rank 2
- What are the key areas being researched?
    - The cloud computing has changed the organizational security costs from capital expenditure cost-model to usage based cost-model. 
    - The review researches how cloud security threats, controls and governance create measurable financial commitments.
- What's widely agreed upon?
    - The review analyzes 59 peer-reviewed articles and it found three cost patterns. Cost-centric attacks, service disruption and recovery expenditures, and defense overhead from encryption and AI-driven detection.  
    - Common findings are that financial commitments variate across the sectors:
       - Banking and healthcare are under heavy complience costs.
       - SMEs are under affordability constraints.
    - Review suggests that four-category taxonomy should be used to manage costs:
       - Resource efficiency management.
       - Investment decision management.
       - Cost-as-governance.
       - post-incident cost classification.
- What are the open questions?
    - Lack of explicit cost modeling frameworks:
       - The studies concentrate mainly on technical performance. Security models need to be linked to measurable economic variables.  
    - Insufficient quantification of governance costs:
       - Research accounts deployment costs, but overlooks continuous costs for validation, monitoring, auditing, and compliance. These activities represent large portion of cloud security expenditure.  
    - Lack of longitudinal empirical evidence:
       - The literature mainly concentrates on simulations or conceptual models. This offers only limited data on long-term economic outcomes. The study needs real-world and time-based studies to estimate how security investments affect resilience and sustainability. 
## C) Threat modeling quickrun
- Company:
   - Kuntokeskus Liikku Oy - Finland wide fitness club chain operating on a low-treshold and mainly unmanned self-service model. In 2025 Liikku Oys revenue was 28 million euros. Heavily reliant on website that customers can manage their membership and on keycard access that customers can enter the gyms.   
- Crown jewels 
    - Data:
       - Customer registry (names, personal identity codes, dates of birth, addresses, email addresses and phone numbers).
       - Payment and billing information (recurring payment agreements, debit/e-invoice records).
       - Access logs and check-in records (timestamps of who enters which gym and when).
    - Business continuity
       - Access control and automated door control systems
       - Member management website and mobile app (door opening via keycard and membership management via website/mobile app) 
    - Reputation
       - Customers trust in seamless, secure and self-accessible gym.
       - Privacy compliance(Leaked GDPR related information could lead to severe reputational damage).   
    - Money
       - Monthly recurring revenue from active memberships.
       - Liquid assets and operational bank balances. 
- Bad guys (Who are they and what do they want?)
   - Ransomware Groups: Seek financial payment by extortion. Encrypts critical databases or threatening public release of extracted data. 
   - Cybercriminals: Seek customer identys and financial details to sell on dark web. 
- How are they going to get it?
  -  Phishing targeting main office staff or regional offices to gain access to network.
  -  Exploit vulnerabilities in cloud APIs or in branch office IoT hardware.
  -  Supply-chain targeting third-party software vendors. 
- Why Liikku Oy? (COI - Capability, Opportunity, Intent)
   - Capability: Ransomware groups/experienced cybercriminals have possibility to use automated scanners which scan vulnerabilities in Liikku Oy systems. 
   - Opportunity: 74 gyms in Finland and all most of the time without staff members. IoT hardware combined with cloud services give a broad attack surface. 
   - Itent: Financial gain - Liikku Oy is not political target, but mainly unstaffed gym chain cannot tolerate long downtimes making it viable target for extortion. 
- Risks
   - Risk 1: Ransomware attack on access control system
     - Likelihood: 15%
     - Impact: €350 000 (All 74 gyms closed for 4 days. Customer compensations, incident reponse & recovery)
     - Estimated value: 15% × €350 000 = €52 500
   - Risk 2: Customer registry breach and data leak
      - Likelihood: 20% 
      - Impact: €180 000 (Regulatory/legal fines, investigation and notifications, and brand damage )
      - Estimated value 20% × €180 000 = €36 000  
- META of Risk 1
   - Mitigate:
     - Network segmentation to isolate IoT network(Door controllers) from office network with firewalls and VLANs
     - Failover mechanism on gym door readers. Doors remain working with cached credentials even if the access control server is down.
     - Enforce MFA on all admin access points, EDR enabled for all devices, and immutable offline backups maintained.
   - Eliminate:
     - Not possible. Completely removing digital access control would require manual physical keys or on-site staff. This would change Liikku Oys whole business model and identity as automated low-cost operating business. 
   - Transfer:
     - Acquire a cyber insurance policy that covers up to €500 000 regarding business interruption, incident forensics, and extortion claims. 
   - Accept:
     - Accept residual risk that is left after mitigations and cyber insurance policy have been implemented.
