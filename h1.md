## B) Review the review
Review: The economic dimensions of cloud security: A systematic literature review - Jufo rank 2
- What are the key areas being researched?
    - The cloud computing has changed the organizational security costs from capital expenditure cost-model to usage based cost-model. 
    - The review researches how cloud security threats, controls and governance create measurable financial commitments.
- What's widely agreed upon?
    - The review analyzes 59 peer-reviewed articles and it found three cost patterns. Cost-centric attacks, service disruption and recovery expenditures, and defense overhead from encryption and AI-driven detection.  
    - Common findings are that financial commitments variate across the sectors:
       - Banking and healthcare are under heavy complience costs.
       - SMEs are under affordability constraints.
    - Review suggests that four-category taxonomy should be used to manage costs:
       - Resource efficiency management.
       - Investment decision management.
       - Cost-as-governance.
       - post-incident cost classification.
- What are the open questions?
    - Lack of explicit cost modeling frameworks:
       - The studies concentrate mainly on technical performance. Security models need to be linked to measurable economic variables.  
    - Insufficient quantification of governance costs:
       - Research accounts deployment costs, but overlooks continuous costs for validation, monitoring, auditing, and compliance. These activities represent large portion of cloud security expenditure.  
    - Lack of longitudinal empirical evidence:
       - The literature mainly concentrates on simulations or conceptual models. This offers only limited data on long-term economic outcomes. The study needs real-world and time-based studies to estimate how security investments affect resilience and sustainability.
