## a) Podcast summary

Beirut bankjob -  https://darknetdiaries.com/episode/6/

- Jayson E. Street was hired to do physical security check to banks office in Beirut
- The goal was to test security of the bank. Gain access to employees computers, steal devices and get networks access via social engineering without any physical break in.
- The test was successfull and concerning part was that it was too easy. He got access behind the counters, installed his own devices to network and could steal banks property without any one questioning him.
- Own pondering: The IT security(firewalls, encryption etc.) is one thing, but they are in vain if the physical security is not in check. This could be fixed on multiple levels: 1. Fix the physical security 2. Access to devices / network devices ports need to be behind locked cabinets. 3. Install solution that blocks unknown devices from the network and VLAN segmentation.  

## b) Blog read

- Source: Krebs on Security: Canadian Man Arrested in Snowflake Data Extortions (https://krebsonsecurity.com/2024/11/canadian-man-arrested-in-snowflake-data-extortions/)

- Hackers used stolen credentials from infostealer malware to access Snowflake cloud databases of many of major companies.

- There was no vulnerability in the platform. The victim accounts simply were without MFA, SSO integration, and network restrictions.

- Attackers extracted massive amounts of customer data and extorted victims by threatening to leak it.

- Own pondering: People can easily forget the shared responsibility in cloud services. The platform itself can be totally secure, but if basic identity and access management is forgotten, you are basically leaving the front door wide open.

## c) link to theory

- Paper: The economic dimensions of cloud security: A systematic literature review (Computers & Security, 2026, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cose.2026.105139)

- The study examines economic models in cloud security and how failing to properly account for shared responsibility creates hidden financial risks for organizations.

- The Snowflake incident could be connected to this. Companies move to the cloud seeking cost savings, but saving on security controls (Enforcement of MFA and monitoring) can lead to massive breach and extortion costs later.

- Own pondering: Cloud security is not just a technical issue, but a risk and cost management problem. The "savings" made by skipping security controls when the system is setup will vanish in to air when an incidents hits.
